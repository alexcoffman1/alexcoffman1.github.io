Software for bail

I build software for bail bondsmen.

People usually pause after that sentence. Bail is old work. It happens in county jails and small offices, on nights and weekends, between a phone call and a decision someone has to stand behind. The industry does not look like a software market from the outside. From the inside, the gap is obvious.

A bondsman needs to know who they have out, what they are on the hook for, and what happens next. That sounds simple. In practice the information lives in paper files, text threads, a spreadsheet, and a system that was built for a different job and then pointed at this one.

I started BailHQ because I thought that was a bad way to run a book of business. We are based in Dallas, close to the people who actually post bonds. You cannot learn this trade from a distance. The customer is not “legal tech.” It is a person who has to decide, tonight, whether they can write the bond.

The modern stack, if the phrase means anything here, is the smallest set of tools that works at the jail and in the parking lot. If it only works at a desk, it is not finished. Features that impress a demo and fail at eleven o’clock are just clutter.

This is unfashionable software. That is fine with me. Specific problems are the ones worth sitting with.
